# Intellectual-property source boundary

This feature is a safety router, not a lawyer or automatic takedown system.

It follows three simple ideas from official sources:

- A rights owner may control uses such as copying and distribution, but permission may also come from a licence.
- Copyright limitations and exceptions vary by country. Examples can include quotation, news, education, research, accessibility, criticism, and parody.
- In the United States, fair use is a case-by-case, fact-specific test. No fixed word count, percentage, or machine score proves the answer.

Because of that, the tool only proposes a category for qualified human review when evidence describes both a protected item and an unauthorized rights-impacting act. It does not treat a title, logo, style, watermark, similarity, or ownership allegation as proof. Unclear licence, ownership, jurisdiction, provenance, or exception questions go to `Uncertain` review.

Official references:

- [WIPO copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)
- [WIPO limitations and exceptions](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/limitations/index)
- [U.S. Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)

The frozen candidate uses synthetic descriptions and metadata only. It contains no copied films, books, music, images, software, pirated payloads, private claimant documents, or individual holdout predictions.
